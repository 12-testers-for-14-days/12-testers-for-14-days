How to Get 12 Testers for a Paid App (100% Free Redeem Code Method)

Google Play does not let testers install paid apps for free on closed testing tracks. This guide shows you the 100% off promo code method first, then two backup options — with step-by-step setup.

Published October 8, 2026 · 15 min read · 12 Testers for 14 Days QA Team
Quick Answer

Generate 100% off promo codes in Play Console (Monetize with Play → Promo codes), then share one code per tester along with your closed testing opt-in link. Testers redeem the code in the Play Store and install for free. If codes fail, fall back to a temporary $0 sale.

You built a paid app. You priced it at $4.99. And now Google says you cannot publish until 12 testers have been opted in for 14 continuous days.

There is one problem: Google Play does not let testers install paid apps for free on closed testing tracks. By default, testers would have to pay the full price just to participate.

This guide walks through every working method to let testers access your paid app for free during closed testing — starting with the 100% off promo code method, which is the most trackable and professional option.

Why Paid Apps Are Different on Closed Testing

Google's own documentation states plainly:

Google's official position

"Testers must purchase paid apps when participating in open or closed tests. For internal tests, testers can install paid apps for free."

Source: Google Play Console Help – Set up an open, closed, or internal test

This means:

  • Internal testing — Testers install paid apps for free. But internal testing does not count toward the 12-tester, 14-day requirement.
  • Closed testing — Testers must pay unless you use a documented workaround.
  • Open testing — Same as closed: testers pay unless you use a workaround.

The workarounds exist. Google just does not advertise them loudly. Here are the three that work in 2026.

Method 1: 100% Off Promo Codes (Recommended)

Promo codes are Google Play's official way to give paid apps away for free. You generate a batch of codes in Play Console, share one per tester, and they redeem it in the Play Store for a 100% discount.

This is the most trackable method — you know exactly who redeemed which code — and it is the closest to a "professional" setup.

Step 1

Open Play Console and select your app

Log in to Google Play Console and choose your paid app from the All Apps list.

Step 2

Navigate to Promo codes

In the left sidebar, go to Monetize with Play → Promo codes.

You will see a button labeled Create promo code.

Step 3

Fill in the promotion details

FieldWhat to Enter
Name"Closed Testing Access – [Your App Name]"
Start dateToday or the day testing begins
End date14+ days after testing begins (16 days recommended)
Number of codes15–20 (buffer for dropouts and failed redemptions)
Step 4

Select promotion type and set 100% discount

Choose "One-time product" — this is for paid apps (not subscriptions).

Set the discount to 100%. This is the critical step. The code must make the app free.

Step 5

Generate and download the codes

Click Create. Google will generate the codes and let you download them as a CSV file.

Each code is unique and can only be redeemed once.

Step 6

Distribute the codes to your testers

Send each tester:

  1. Your closed testing opt-in link (from Play Console → Testing → Closed testing → Testers)
  2. A unique promo code from your CSV

Include instructions for redemption (see below).

How testers redeem the promo code

  1. Open the opt-in link on their Android device
  2. Click "Become a tester"
  3. Open the Google Play Store app
  4. Tap their profile icon → Payments & subscriptions → Redeem code
  5. Enter the promo code
  6. Install your app for free

Edge case: promo codes and closed testing

Google has never officially documented whether a promo code is redeemable for a paid app whose only active track is closed testing. Many developers report success; some report the code fails until the app has a production or open testing track active.

If a code fails to redeem: fall back to Method 2 ($0 sale), which is fully documented and reliable.

Do not add testers to the License Testers list

License testers use test payment methods. This conflicts with promo code redemption and can cause "invalid code" errors. Keep testers off the License Testers list when using promo codes.

Method 2: Temporary $0 Sale (Most Reliable)

If promo codes fail or you want a foolproof method, run a $0 sale. This is Google's own documented way to temporarily make a paid app free.

Google's official statement on $0 sales

"You can enter 0 if you want to temporarily offer your app as a $0 sale, which will automatically set all country/region prices to 0 as well. A $0 sale is temporary and is not the same as permanently offering your app for free."

Source: Google Play Console Help – Create sales for paid apps

Step-by-step: Create a $0 sale

Step 1

Open Play Console and select your app

Log in and choose your paid app.

Step 2

Navigate to App Pricing

Go to Monetize with Play → Products → App Pricing.

Step 3

Find the Sales tab

Click the Sales tab. This is where you manage promotional pricing.

Step 4

Create a new sale

Click Create sale. Fill in:

  • Sale name — "Closed Testing Free Access"
  • Start date — the day testers should start
  • End date — at least 16 days after the start (14 days testing + 2 days buffer)
  • Price — set to $0.00
Step 5

Save and activate

Click Save. The sale will activate on the start date you set. Testers can then install your app for free during the sale window.

Do not forget to end the sale

If you leave a $0 sale running, your app stays free indefinitely. Set a calendar reminder for day 15 or 16 to end the sale. The sale ending does not remove the app from testers who already installed it.

Method 3: Free Clone (For Pre-Testing Only)

If you only need testers for internal testing (not the formal closed testing requirement), Google states that testers can install paid apps for free on internal tracks.

Important limitation

Internal testing does not count toward the 12-tester, 14-day requirement. That requirement applies specifically to closed testing. A free clone is useful for early smoke testing before your closed test, but it will not satisfy Google's production access gate.

You can also create a separate free version of your app (a "clone") and upload it to a closed testing track. This requires maintaining two app listings, two packages, and two sets of updates — which is why most developers prefer promo codes or a $0 sale.

What to tell your testers

Be upfront about the paid app situation. Use this template:

"I'm running a closed test for my paid app [App Name]. The app normally costs $X.XX, but I'm giving testers free access via a 100% off promo code. All I need is for you to install it, use it a few times over the next 14 days, and share any feedback. You keep the app for free after the test ends."

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting to end the $0 sale — Set a calendar reminder for day 15. Otherwise your app stays free forever.
  • Generating too few promo codes — Need 12 testers? Generate 15–20 codes. Testers drop out, codes fail, buffer is essential.
  • Adding testers to the License Testers list — This conflicts with promo code redemption. Keep testers off that list.
  • Assuming internal testing counts — Internal testing does not satisfy the 12-tester requirement. You must use closed testing.
  • Not having a feedback channel ready — Google expects testers to actually engage. Set up a Google Form, Discord channel, or in-app feedback button before the test starts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do 100% off promo codes work for paid apps in closed testing? +

Google has not officially documented whether promo codes are redeemable for a paid app whose only active track is closed testing. Many developers report success. If a code fails to redeem, fall back to the $0 sale method, which is fully documented and reliable.

Can testers install a paid app for free on closed testing? +

By default, testers must purchase paid apps in open or closed tests. Google's documented workarounds are a temporary $0 sale or individual promo codes. Internal testing is the only track where paid apps are free by default, but internal testing does not satisfy the 12-tester requirement.

How long should a $0 sale last for closed testing? +

Set the sale for at least 16 days — 14 days for testing plus a 2-day buffer for the Play Console counter to update. Set a calendar reminder to end the sale so your app returns to its original price.

What if testers cannot redeem the promo code? +

Common causes: the tester is on your License Testers list (which conflicts with promo code redemption), the code has expired, or the app's only active track is closed testing. Remove the tester from License Testers and retry, or switch to a $0 sale.

Do testers keep the paid app after the test ends? +

Yes. Testers who install during a $0 sale or redeem a 100% off promo code retain access to the app after the sale or code ends. This is standard Google Play behavior.

Can I use promo codes for a subscription app? +

Promo codes for subscriptions offer free trials, not free subscriptions. They do not extend existing free trials. If you have a subscription app, use the $0 sale method or internal testing.

Bottom Line

Summary

Paid apps can pass Google Play's 12-tester, 14-day requirement. You just need to give testers free access through one of Google's official methods.

The most trackable path: Generate 100% off promo codes, distribute one per tester, and have them redeem in the Play Store.

The most reliable path: Run a temporary $0 sale for 16+ days. It is fully documented by Google and has no edge cases.

Your testers get a free app. You get production access. Google gets evidence that your app works.

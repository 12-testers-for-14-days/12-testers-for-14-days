How to Recover a Failed Closed Test Without Resetting Everything

TL;DR

If your closed test was rejected for insufficient testing, you do not need to create a new app or a new testing track. Simply recruit a new, reliable pool of 12+ testers, add their emails to the existing track, push a minor version update, and run a fresh 14-day cycle.

Don't Panic: You Don't Need to Start from Scratch

Receiving an "Insufficient Testing" rejection is disheartening, but it is not the end of your app. Many developers mistakenly believe they need to delete their app, change their package name, and create a brand new listing.

This is false. You can reuse your existing closed testing track. You simply need to satisfy the engagement metrics you failed on the first attempt.

Step 1: Clean House

If your previous test failed, it means your previous testers (whether they were friends, family, or cheap internet forums) did not do their job.

  • Go to your Play Console > Testing > Closed testing.
  • Edit your tester email list and remove the unreliable testers.
  • Add a new list of highly reliable, managed QA testers.

Step 2: Push a Version Update

To signal to Google's algorithm that a fresh testing cycle is beginning, you should push a new app bundle to your closed testing track.

Increment your version code
Change your app version from 1.0.0 to 1.0.1. You don't need to add major new features; updating a text string or fixing a minor layout issue is sufficient.
Release to the track
Upload this new bundle to your existing Closed Testing track and roll out the release.

Step 3: Execute a Flawless 14-Day Cycle

Once your new release is available, instruct your new testers to opt-in and download the update. From that day forward, ensure that there is daily active usage, varied session lengths, and that you collect specific UI/UX feedback to use in your next production application.
